GXP GATEWAY AUDIT

Your AI, APIs and gateways touch lab systems.
Who keeps the audit trail?

GxP Gateway Audit (package mcp-glp-audit-proxy) records every call your AI assistants, integrations and gateways make to your systems, in a tamper-evident, fail-closed audit trail you host yourself. It complements your systems' own audit trail and is designed to support GLP/GxP, 21 CFR Part 11 and EU GMP Annex 11 workflows.

What you get

Use AI and automation on regulated data without losing track of who did what, when and why.

01

A complete record of every call

Who called which system, what was done, when, and the reason given, written at the moment of the call.

02

Tampering is detectable

Records are hash-chained. If one is altered, removed or reordered, verification shows exactly where.

03

Nothing goes unrecorded

Fail-closed: if a record cannot be written, the call does not go through, and you are alerted.

04

Human approval when you need it

Optionally require an approver to approve, review or reject a call before it is authorised.

05

Ready for inspections and audits

Search and export the trail, run review-by-exception, and produce an offline regulatory report of your configuration.

06

Easy to add

Runs as a reverse proxy in front of what you already have: clients only change their base URL. Always over TLS.

No regulation is Pro-only as a whole: every supported row is supported by the free Community edition. Pro adds features such as record signing, WORM retention, database storage and the IQ/OQ pack. Personal electronic signatures under 21 CFR Part 11 are out of scope.

Limitations

  • The gateway records calls, not the internal state of the target system. It complements, and does not replace, the target system's own audit trail (old and new values).
  • In hash-only and redacted modes the trail proves a payload existed and was unaltered, but not its content. Responses are stored only in full mode.
  • A shared bearer token is an access gate, not a unique user identity. Attribution through an end-user header is only as strong as the gateway that authenticated the person.
  • The Community edition is tamper-evident (hash chain), not signed and not tamper-proof. Signing and WORM retention are Pro features.
  • Reason capture can be configured off; doing so removes a control the regulations expect.
  • Timestamps are as accurate as the host clock.
  • The failed-login lockout (listen.auth_failure_limit) is optional and off by default. The configuration report links it to its rule, and the customer's QA decides whether to switch it on.
  • Configuration changes take effect, and are recorded, only when the proxy restarts.

Details by regulation

GxP Gateway Audit is an audit proxy placed in front of MCP tool servers and HTTPS APIs. It writes a computer-generated, time-stamped, hash-chained audit trail of every call. It is designed to support customers' GLP, GMP and Part 11 data-integrity programmes. It has no third-party approval, and using it does not by itself make any system meet these rules. Fitness for use must be established by the customer's own validation, SOPs and reviews.

Texts checked: 25 October 2026, official sources (eCFR, fda.gov, health.ec.europa.eu, oecd.org, one.oecd.org, who.int, fedlex.admin.ch, picscheme.org, gov.uk, ich.org, ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu).

1. FDA 21 CFR Part 11

Requirement: 11.10(e) "Use of secure, computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails to independently record the date and time of operator entries and actions that create, modify, or delete electronic records. Record changes shall not obscure previously recorded information." Also 11.10(b) copies, 11.10(c) protection, 11.10(d) "Limiting system access to authorized individuals", 11.10(g) authority checks.

Designed to support: server-generated UTC timestamps; append-only SHA-256 hash chain with a verify tool that detects altered, removed or reordered entries; fail-closed (no response is returned if the record cannot be written); role-based access (operator, approver, QA read-only, admin); tool and route allow/deny lists; complete electronic export.

Customer responsibility: system validation, clock synchronisation, record retention and backup, user management, and audit trail review. Electronic signatures (11.50, 11.70, 11.100, 11.200, 11.300) are out of scope; the approval feature is not an electronic signature.

2. FDA predicate rules and guidance (21 CFR 58, 211; Part 11 Scope and Application 2003; Data Integrity Q&A 2018)

Requirement: 58.130(e) changes "shall indicate the reason for such change"; Data Integrity Q&A 2018 defines an audit trail as a record that "allows for reconstruction of the course of events" and warns "When login credentials are shared, a unique individual cannot be identified through the login".

Designed to support: a reason is required on write calls by default (X-Audit-Reason header or reason field); per-person identity through one client certificate per person, or through a user id from an authenticated gateway.

Customer responsibility: keep reason capture switched on; do not rely on a shared token alone for attribution.

3. EU GMP Annex 11 (2011) and the 2025 draft revision

Requirement: Annex 11 sec. 9 "For change or deletion of GMP-relevant data the reason should be documented"; 12.4 systems "should be designed to record the identity of operators entering, changing, confirming or deleting data including date and time"; 7.1 "Access to data should be ensured throughout the retention period". The 2025 draft (consultation, not in force) adds who/what/when/why, time zone, audit trail "enabled and locked at all times", and searchable, sortable copies.

Designed to support: reason on writes, operator identity and UTC time in each record, audit that cannot be switched off (attempts are logged), searchable export for QA.

Customer responsibility: risk assessment, periodic audit trail review, retention and archive controls, and capturing old and new values in the target system's own audit trail.

4. OECD GLP Principles (No. 1) and No. 17 Computerised Systems

Requirement: No. 17 para 80 "Any change to electronic records must not obscure the original entry and be time and date stamped and traceable to the person who made the change"; para 95 record "the date, time and identity of operators entering, changing, confirming or deleting data"; No. 1 8.3.5 "Reason for changes should be given."

Designed to support: append-only, time-stamped, attributable records with reason; QA read-only role; admin-only settings; Swiss GLP retention profile (10 years after study completion) with legal hold.

Customer responsibility: test facility SOPs, archive management, and retaining original and modified data in the target system (No. 17 para 83).

5. MHRA GxP Data Integrity (2018) and PIC/S PI 041-1

Requirement: MHRA 6.13 an audit trail records actions "without obscuring or overwriting the original record" and captures "who, what, when and why"; PIC/S 9.6 entries "recorded in true time".

Designed to support: per-call records written at the time of the call, with identity, action, time and reason.

Customer responsibility: data governance, ALCOA+ controls across the whole process, and review.

6. OECD No. 22 Data Integrity, WHO TRS 1033 Annex 4 and the Swiss GLP Ordinance

Requirement: OECD No. 22 6.13 audit trails "should always be switched on during GLP activities", and switching them off and on again "should" be recorded automatically; WHO TRS 1033 Annex 4 11.10 audit trails "should reflect ... users, dates, times, ... changes and reasons for changes ... and enabling and disenabling of audit trails", 11.9 "shared logins or generic user access should not be used"; Swiss GLP Ordinance (SR 813.112.1) Annex 2 sec. 8.3 para 5 audit trails linking changes to persons with a reason, and sec. 10 retention "for at least ten years after study completion".

Designed to support: a record written when auditing starts; configuration changes recorded; per-person identity via client certificates or an authenticated gateway; reason on writes; a 10-year Swiss GLP retention profile.

Customer responsibility: individual user accounts, periodic checks that auditing is running, archive and backup. WHO 11.12 and OECD No. 22 6.13 accept add-on audit software as a justified, documented and time-bound measure for systems without their own audit trail, not as a permanent replacement.

6a. Swiss AGIT Guidelines for the Validation of Computerised Systems (V4.0, 2025)

AGIT guidance is not legally binding; it interprets the GLP Principles for Swiss test facilities. In a GLP test facility the gateway is itself a computerised system that transfers and stores GLP data, so the customer validates it like any configurable system (AGIT category C). The gateway is designed to support that work: an open OQ test runner, a requirements traceability matrix, a configuration report per regulation, release notes, audit-trail search and export for the audit-trail review SOP, and documented system requirements. Backup and restore, contingency planning and the validation itself remain customer procedures.

6b. Swiss AGIT Guidelines for the Archiving of Electronic Raw Data (V3.0, 2026) and for the Acquisition and Processing of Electronic Raw Data (V3.0, 2025)

AGIT guidance is not a legal document. It expects archived electronic raw data and their audit trails to stay complete, unaltered, linked to their metadata and readable for the whole retention period (at least 10 years under the Swiss GLP Ordinance), with an index of archived files, integrity checks at risk-based intervals, tested backups, validated migrations, and disposal only with documented test facility management approval.

Designed to support: plain-text JSONL records readable with standard tools and an open-source verifier; a hash chain and head file that show whether a log is complete and unaltered; UTC timestamps; a 10-year retention profile, legal hold, and no disposal function in the Community edition. Also in Community 0.4.0: an archive manifest (archive-manifest: file list with sizes, hashes, record ranges and versions); verification of an archived copy against that manifest (verify --manifest) with a dated check report; closing a log segment requires a reason, a ticket and an approver, all recorded; offline runs (verify, export, manifest, report, review) are written to a separate hash-chained run log, checked with verify-run-log, and never append to the live audit log.

Customer responsibility: the archivist, archive storage on read-only or WORM media, off-site backups, clock synchronisation, control of the gateway configuration, periodic integrity checks, migration and disposal procedures. The gateway records gateway activity; it complements, and does not replace, the data audit trail of the target system.

7. EU AI Act (only for high-risk AI systems)

Requirement: Art. 12(1) high-risk AI systems "shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs)"; Art. 19(1) and 26(6) logs kept "of at least six months, unless provided otherwise in applicable Union or national law".

Designed to support: automatic logs of every tool or API call that a provider or deployer may keep; a 6-month default retention floor. The gateway does not by itself make an AI system meet Art. 12; whether the AI Act applies depends on the customer's system.

8. GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679)

Every audit record holds personal data (user id and name, client certificate fingerprint, request path, kept headers), whatever the body mode. The body mode is the customer's choice and applies to both MCP and HTTPS traffic: the default hash_only stores only SHA-256 hashes of bodies, while full stores request and response bodies, which may include personal data. A SHA-256 of a short body can be re-identified by guessing, so hash_only is not anonymisation. The customer is the controller and decides the lawful basis, the body mode, retention, and how to answer erasure requests. Records are append-only and hash-chained: removing one breaks verification, so legal retention duties (GDPR Art. 17(3)) and erasure must be weighed by the customer before choosing full mode. The Community edition does not encrypt the log at rest; use disk encryption. A table of the personal data in each record is provided for the customer's record of processing (Art. 30).

6c. OECD No. 15 (archives), No. 25 (IT security) and No. 17 Supplement 1 (cloud)

These OECD advisory documents expect archived electronic records to be locked so they cannot be altered or deleted without detection, transfers documented with a chain of custody, migrations validated, accounts locked after repeated failed sign-ins, backups kept apart from the original data, and controls in cloud settings that the test facility runs independently of the provider.

Designed to support: a hash chain and head file that make alteration or deletion detectable; verification that the test facility can run itself against a head value it keeps on another system; TLS 1.3 for remote access; recorded failed authentication attempts. Also in Community 0.4.0: an archive manifest with custody records (archive-transfer) and a receipt that is added only when the received package matches (verify --manifest --received-by); an optional limit on repeated failed authentication attempts (listen.auth_failure_limit), off unless configured.

Customer responsibility: read-only or WORM archive storage, off-site backups of logs and configuration, restore tests, separation of system administration from data ownership, and service agreements with any hosting provider.

6d. EMA guideline on computerised systems and electronic data in clinical trials (2023), PIC/S PI 011-3 and AGIT change management and IT service provider guidelines

The EMA guideline expects changes to user roles and permissions to be recorded, timestamps to be unambiguous, the audit trail to be exportable as a searchable data file, the generation of data extracts and exports to be traceable, and audit trail review to be risk-based. AGIT expects software upgrades to be impact-assessed against the affected requirements.

Designed to support: UTC timestamps; recorded configuration at every start; audit-trail search and export. Also in Community 0.4.0: each startup record holds the identities and roles, the body mode and a SHA-256 of the effective configuration, and lists what changed since the previous start (with an optional change reason); a review-by-exception report (review) with JSON, Markdown or CSV output; a record of each offline export and verification run in a separate hash-chained run log; release notes that name the affected requirements and the effect on existing records; and an OQ runner option (--requirement) that repeats only the tests mapped to named requirements.

The gateway's hash chain is kept on the customer's system. It is not a distributed or independent ledger.

How it works

Three steps, no changes to your target systems.

Clients, AI assistants, gateways→GxP Gateway Audit (MCP / HTTPS, always TLS)→MCP servers, APIs, LLM endpoints
1. CONNECT

Point clients at the proxy

Only the base URL changes. Authentication by mTLS and/or token.

2. RECORD

Every call is written to the trail

Who, what, when and why, with the request body stored the way you choose: hash only (default), redacted or full.

3. VERIFY

Check and review at any time

If a record is deleted or the chain is broken, it stops and alerts, saying which record and when. Review and archive tools help QA.

Technical details (for IT and validation teams)
  • Audits MCP and HTTPS channels, six flows: mcp2mcp, mcp2api, api2mcp, api2api, mcp2llm, api2llm.
  • Reverse proxy, always TLS; authentication by mTLS and/or token.
  • Every record is RFC 8785 canonical JSON, hash-chained with SHA-256 (audit schema 1.3.0). Fail-closed hash chain with alerts.
  • Optional human approval: an approver records approved, reviewed or rejected with glp_approve (MCP) or POST /_glp/approve (HTTPS); only approved authorises the call.
  • Body storage: hash only (default), redacted or full.
  • Archive manifests, a review command, and a hash-chained run log for offline runs.
  • Offline regulatory report: lists regulations by type and country, says for each whether your current configuration supports it and names the parameter to change if it does not; it writes its own hash-chained run log.
  • Open traceability matrix and OQ runner. 240 automated tests.

These materials support your own validation process; your organisation remains responsible for validating its use.
